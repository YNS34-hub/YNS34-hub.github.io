About

I am a master’s student in mathematics studying nonlinear partial differential equations at the School of Science, Harbin University of Science and Technology.

My research interests include nonlinear elliptic equations, Orlicz growth, rearrangement methods, symmetrization, comparison principles, and nonlinear analysis.

Alongside mathematics, I examine how AI can augment rigorous human reasoning. I use AI tools to question proof structure, identify hidden assumptions, improve academic English, assist with coding, and design structured research-review workflows.

The goal is not to replace mathematical reasoning, but to understand how human judgment and machine intelligence can complement each other in scientific discovery.

Research

Research focus

Nonlinear elliptic partial differential equations

These equations describe equilibrium states in systems whose response is not proportional to the input. I study what can be proved about their solutions when the growth law is more general than a standard power and when lower-order effects also depend on the gradient.

Research question

When do solutions exist—and how can they be compared?

The central problem is to establish reliable existence and comparison results under nonlinear growth assumptions without losing control of the gradient-dependent terms.

Methods

Transform the geometry, preserve the information.

  • Rearrangement methods
  • Symmetrization
  • Comparison principles
  • Orlicz-space analysis

Current focus

Graduate research project · manuscript in revision

Nonlinear elliptic problems with Orlicz growth and gradient-dependent lower-order terms, including proof development, verification, LaTeX preparation, literature review, and reviewer-response revision.

Technical layer Open research note

−div(a(x,u,∇u)) = H(x,u,∇u)

I work with nonlinear Dirichlet problems whose principal operator follows general Orlicz-type growth, while the right-hand side may contain lower-order terms depending on ∇u. Rearrangement and comparison techniques provide a way to relate solutions to more symmetric model problems and obtain usable estimates.

AI & Research

A second reader, not an oracle

AI should augment rigorous human reasoning rather than replace it.

Mathematical work is useful precisely because its claims can be checked. AI can widen the conversation around a proof, but fluency is not evidence and confidence is not validity.

In practice, I use Claude and other AI tools for

  • 01 Mathematical dialogue and proof-structure checks
  • 02 Hidden assumptions and implication testing
  • 03 Possible counterexample exploration
  • 04 Academic English and manuscript structure
  • 05 Coding assistance and research workflow design

Thinking

Better questions, not just faster answers

Scientific value begins before the proof.

Today, mathematicians often solve questions already posed and prove conjectures already formulated. As AI systems become stronger, part of the human contribution may shift toward finding meaningful problems, asking deeper questions, and choosing directions worth pursuing.

AI may help us reach answers faster. Human flourishing may depend on becoming better at deciding which questions are worth asking.
