The problem setting

I study nonlinear elliptic equations: equations used to describe equilibrium problems in which the response need not be proportional to the input. My focus is on more general growth laws and on lower-order terms involving the gradient of the solution.

A model form for the problems in this area is

−ΔAu=β|∇u|q+f(x)A model equation; the full hypotheses belong to the research.

The principal operator encodes a growth law, while the gradient term introduces a further source of nonlinearity. Orlicz and Orlicz–Sobolev spaces provide a setting for working beyond a single power-growth model.

The methods

My research interests include rearrangement, symmetrization, comparison principles and nonlinear analysis. These methods ask how a problem can be related to a more symmetric one while retaining information useful for estimates.

Rearrangement
Reorganizing a function through the distribution of its values, rather than treating every geometric detail in the same way.
Comparison
Relating a solution to a more tractable model under stated assumptions. The assumptions are part of the result, not a technical afterthought.
Existence
Identifying a setting in which a solution can be established, alongside the regularity and admissibility needed to use it.

Current work

My graduate work concerns elliptic problems with Orlicz growth and gradient-dependent lower-order terms, with particular attention to existence and rearrangement comparison.

The work around the mathematics includes proof development, checking dependencies and assumptions, preparing LaTeX manuscripts, reviewing related literature and revising arguments.
