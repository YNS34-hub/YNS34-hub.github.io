Start from the question.

A manuscript revision involves more than improving individual sentences. A change to a definition can affect a lemma; a response to a reviewer can depend on a hypothesis stated elsewhere.

This prototype is designed around a reviewer-led audit: make the question explicit, connect it to the relevant evidence, trace the affected reasoning and leave the final decision to the author.

An inspectable workflow

The walkthrough below explains the intended process. It does not connect to a model, upload a manuscript or claim to validate a proof.

Begin with scope

What, exactly,
needs to be resolved?

Separate the reviewer’s question from the proposed revision. Identify the mathematical claim, the relevant assumptions and the part of the manuscript in scope.

Expected outputA specific audit question, not a general request to “check the proof.”

Make the basis visible

Which passage
supports the response?

Connect the question to exact definitions, statements, proof steps or references. Keep the supporting text available alongside the proposed response.

Expected outputAn evidence trail that a human can inspect and correct.

Follow the implications

What else changes
when this changes?

Track which later statements depend on the revised assumption or argument. Flag possible semantic changes separately from language improvements.

Expected outputA set of dependent claims and checks, rather than an isolated edit.

Keep judgment human

What has actually
been established?

Present the question, evidence and unresolved issues for human review. Acceptance, further revision and rejection remain explicit author decisions.

Expected outputA recorded decision with reasons, not an automatic certificate of correctness.

My role

I conceived and specified the workflow around the needs of mathematical revision. My work includes problem definition, system and workflow design, verification boundaries, AI-agent-assisted implementation, and test and acceptance criteria.

The project remains a private prototype. Implementation with AI assistance is not presented here as evidence that the system has been independently validated.

The boundaries matter.

What the project is intended to support

Reviewer-question tracking, manuscript evidence, dependency checks, semantic-preservation checks and staged quality assurance before human sign-off.

What it does not establish

It is not a mathematical truth engine, a replacement for proof, a production-validated service or a claim that every error will be detected.

What is public here

The purpose, design and limits of the workflow. Private manuscript material, reviewer correspondence and prototype source code are not exposed on this site.
