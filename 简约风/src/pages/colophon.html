Content before components.

The structure follows two real threads: graduate research in nonlinear equations, and experiments in the tools around mathematical work. There is no placeholder publication archive, client list or collection of invented project outcomes.

Large serif type, smaller utilitarian text, open space and rules do most of the visual work. The color palette stays close to paper and botanical ink. Type uses system fonts, so reading does not wait for a font download.

One idea that moves.

The homepage figure begins with 48 positive samples of a specified function. It places those same samples in decreasing order. Sample heights remain fixed throughout; their horizontal positions change.

u(x) = 0.55 + 0.27 sin(2πx) + 0.13 cos(6πx)

The sample points are x = (i + ½)/48 for i = 0, …, 47. It is a discrete illustration, not experimental data, a numerical PDE solution or a proof of a comparison theorem.

Desktop scrolling can control the arrangement. Smaller screens use an explicit slider, and reduced-motion mode removes automatic movement and pinned reading. The content is available in every version.

A small, direct website.

The website uses semantic HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with a small dependency-free build script. It does not load a WebGL engine, an animation framework, third-party fonts or a content-management runtime.

The design and implementation were developed with AI assistance. The biographical details and project descriptions are grounded in Benjamin’s supplied context and existing website; the project status is stated rather than embellished.

Privacy & access

This build contains no analytics, advertising, tracking pixels, cookies or contact-form backend. An optional motion preference is stored locally in your browser. External profile links and your email application are separate services.

Navigation and reading work without JavaScript. Interactive controls support a keyboard, and the site follows your device’s reduced-motion preference. The footer also provides an explicit motion toggle.

Hosting providers and external services may handle connection data according to their own policies. This page describes the website code, not those services.
