The Memory Palace

I am a master's student in mathematics at the School of Science, Harbin University of Science and Technology. I study nonlinear elliptic partial differential equations, Orlicz growth, rearrangement methods and comparison principles. Alongside mathematics, I explore how AI can assist rigorous human reasoning and how research can become an interactive spatial experience.

research

  • Nonlinear Elliptic Problems — Existence, growth and comparison.

    My graduate research concerns nonlinear Dirichlet problems with general Orlicz-type growth and gradient-dependent lower-order terms. The current manuscript is in revision. The work includes proof development, verification, LaTeX preparation, literature review and reviewer-response revision. This exhibit summarizes an active research direction; it does not claim an accepted paper or a published theorem.

  • Rearrangement & Symmetry — Transform the geometry. Preserve the information.

    A spatial study of rearrangement and symmetrization, two methods used in my research. A function's level sets can be reorganized into a symmetric model while preserving their distribution. The resulting geometry offers a way to reason about estimates and compare nonlinear solutions. The sculpture is an illustrative visualization, not a proof of a new result.

  • Beyond Power Growth — When one exponent is not enough.

    An introduction to the general growth laws behind Orlicz spaces. Rather than assuming every response follows one power, a convex growth function describes a wider range of behavior. This methodological exhibit accompanies the active nonlinear elliptic research direction and is not presented as a separately published paper.

experiments

  • Nonlinear Glass Study — A mathematical surface, held in light.

    The original portfolio's genuine WebGL nonlinear glass surface is preserved with its geometry, local assets and interactive rotation. Displaced geometry, physical transmission and environment lighting make the silhouette and refraction change in three dimensions. The palace's glass core continues this visual research.

  • The Review Lens — Question → challenge → verify → refine → human judgment.

    The original five-step AI-assisted mathematical review workflow, retained as an interactive conceptual exhibit. AI observations are prompts for verification. The researcher checks each claim, decides what belongs in the manuscript and retains final mathematical judgment. There is no live model integration in this exhibit.

  • Memory Architecture — The same door. A changing relationship.

    A new palace-specific creative coding experiment: procedural rooms have stable identities while local visits alter small details. The world keeps a browser-local memory of your exploration. No account or server is involved. Clearing browser storage returns the rooms to their first encounter.
